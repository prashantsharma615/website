Why Knowing Your Skin Type Matters

Skincare is not one-size-fits-all. When you understand your skin's biological tendencies, you can nourish it effectively without causing irritation or imbalance.

Targeted Active Ingredients

Prevent counterproductive reactions. Applying heavy occlusives to oily skin leads to clogged pores, while harsh exfoliants on dry or sensitive skin compromise your skin barrier.

Moisture Barrier Protection

Your skin barrier defends against bacteria, pollution, and water loss. Knowing your type preserves this delicate lipid barrier, keeping your complexion resilient and calm.

Eliminate Wasted Spending

Stop guessing in cosmetic aisles. When you choose formulations aligned with your skin type, your products deliver visible results rather than sitting unused on your shelf.

Balanced Day-to-Night Care

Understand how environmental stressors and daytime activities change your skin's needs, empowering you to adjust daytime protection and nighttime restoration appropriately.

The Five Core Classifications

Understanding The Primary Skin Types

Every complexion falls along a spectrum of sebum production, hydration capacity, and cellular reactivity.

Oily

Oily Complexion

Produces excess sebum across the face, often characterized by visible pores, persistent shine, and a tendency toward congestion or blackheads.

Dry

Dry Complexion

Lacks adequate natural lipids and moisture, often feeling tight or rough after washing, with potential flakiness, dullness, or fine dehydration lines.

Combination

Combination Complexion

Features an oily T-zone (forehead, nose, and chin) alongside normal or dry cheeks, requiring a balanced multi-zone approach to care.

Normal

Normal Complexion

Well-balanced lipid and water balance, neither overly greasy nor persistently dry, with fine pores, smooth texture, and strong barrier resilience.

Reactive Quality

Sensitive Skin

Easily irritated, reactive to new formulations, temperatures, or fragrances. Sensitive skin can co-occur with any primary skin type.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my skin type change over time? +

Yes. While genetics provide the underlying baseline for sebum production, skin behavior shifts with aging, seasons, humidity, hormonal fluctuations, stress levels, and diet. It is common to experience drier skin during winter or as we age, and increased oiliness in hot, humid climates.

What is the difference between dry skin and dehydrated skin? +

Dry skin is a skin type lacking in natural oil (lipids), causing a compromised lipid barrier. Dehydrated skin is a temporary condition lacking in water, which can happen to any skin type—even very oily skin. Hydrating serums (like hyaluronic acid and glycerin) address dehydration, while facial oils and rich ceramides address dryness.

Why is sensitivity treated as a separate characteristic? +

Sensitivity refers to the hyperactivity and reactivity of the skin's nerve fibers and barrier permeability. It frequently co-exists with oily skin (e.g., acne-prone yet reactive), dry skin (easily irritated from lipid loss), or combination skin. Recognizing it independently prevents using overly aggressive formulations.

Do I need a complex 10-step skincare routine? +

No. Dermatologists overwhelmingly recommend a simple, consistent routine: a gentle cleanser, hydration/treatment suited to your barrier, a nourishing moisturizer, and daily broad-spectrum SPF 30+. Consistency and skin-appropriate formulations matter far more than routine length.
